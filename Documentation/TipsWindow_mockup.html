Strategy Tips

Tips are ordered from basic to advanced
🃏 Beginner — Understanding the Basics Beginner
  • • Your Hidden cards score face value when played. A King from the Hidden hand scores 13. Simple.
  • • Shown cards follow different rules. A Shown King is only worth 5. A Shown Ace is 10. A Shown Queen is 14 — the highest score in the game. Learn these before planning your plays.
  • • The final score uses face value only, regardless of which hand the card was played from. Win tricks to claim prizes; prizes are worth exactly what's printed on the card.
  • • You don't choose which card gets revealed — that's random. But you do choose whose hand to target. That choice matters a great deal.
⚡ Intermediate — Playing with Intent Intermediate
  • • Target reveals wisely. Moving a card from Hidden to Shown can dramatically change its play score. A hidden Ace (14) becomes a Shown Ace (10). A hidden Queen (12) becomes a Shown Queen (14) — potentially making an opponent's card more dangerous.
  • • Disrupt the leader. Target reveals at whoever is currently ahead in points. Exposing one of their key cards limits how they can play it and may force bad decisions.
  • • Expose players with few hidden cards. Once a player's entire hand is Shown, all their remaining cards are public knowledge and you can outmanoeuvre them with near-perfect information.
  • • When leading a trick, play a mid-value card. Leading your best card signals your strength and hands the trick to anyone with a slightly better card. A mid card baits opponents into committing without giving too much away.
  • • Ties are a weapon. If you can't beat the current leader but can match their score, you deny them the trick entirely — both tied cards are discarded.
🧠 Advanced — Card Counting & Precision Advanced
  • • Track what's been discarded. 52 cards, 4 of each rank. As the game progresses you can infer which high-value cards are still in play — and whose hands they're likely in.
  • • When following, spend the minimum that wins. If the current high card scores 11 and you hold cards scoring 12, 13, and 14 — play the 12. Save the stronger cards for tricks where the margin matters more.
  • • Use the Shown King as a cheap winner. A King from the Shown hand only scores 5. Opponents may not bother burning a strong card to beat it. Spending it to win a modest trick is efficient — and it preserves your high face-value cards for prizes.
  • • When you can't win, protect your prize potential. Throw your lowest face-value card. Even losing, you minimise the prize you hand the winner — and keep your high cards for tricks you can win.
  • • Read the prize pool before deciding. If the remaining cards in the play zone are low-value, intentionally losing isn't a tragedy. Don't burn an Ace to win a trick that only yields a 3♣.
  • • Late-game reveals are more powerful. When players have fewer cards, a single reveal exposes a much larger fraction of someone's remaining hand — giving you near-perfect information for the final tricks.
